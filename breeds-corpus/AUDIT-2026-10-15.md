# Breed corpus audit, 15 October 2026 (Cowork)

Why this exists: Krish wants the chat to feel personal from the first message, and the breed corpus is meant to help Biscuit "know" the dog. This audit checks whether the corpus can do that for the dogs US buyers actually have, and adds the smallest safe fixes. The original files are untouched.

## What was checked

`dog_corpus_completed.csv` (370 canonical rows) and `breed_lookup_normalized.csv` (827 queries), matched with the normalisation rules in `dog_corpus_completed_readme.md`, against 39 names US owners commonly type: the most popular US breeds, their nicknames, the common doodles, and "mutt".

## Findings

1. **9 of 39 common US names found nothing:** Boxer, Chihuahua, Pomeranian, Doberman, Cane Corso, Havanese, German Shorthaired Pointer, Jack Russell and Pit Bull. Most of these breeds are in the corpus under their registry names, which owners don't type:
   - Deutscher Boxer
   - Chihuahueño
   - Deutscher Spitz (the Pomeranian is its Zwergspitz size)
   - Dobermann
   - Cane Corso Italiano
   - Bichon Havanais
   - Deutsch Kurzhaar
2. **Buyer-facing names are in the registry's language** for common breeds: Bouledogue Français, Caniche, Deutscher Schäferhund, Zwergschnauzer, Deutsche Dogge, Berner Sennenhund. Showing these to a US buyer would undo the "it knows my dog" moment.
3. **Many trait bands are wrong for common US breeds:**
   - Beagle, Pembroke Welsh Corgi, Great Dane and Miniature Schnauzer are all "medium-large".
   - The Miniature Schnauzer is a "Guardian".
   - One Poodle row covers toy to standard sizes as "toy-small".
   - The Afghan Hound is a "scent hound" although its FCI group is 10 (sighthounds).
   - The Cavalier King Charles Spaniel is a "flushing dog" and a "Working Goofball".
   - 294 of 370 rows have "Working Goofball" as their secondary type.
4. **The ids of accented names are mangled** (`bouledogue-fran-ais`, `chihuahue-o`, `deutscher-sch-ferhund`). They work as keys, but must never be shown.
5. **There is no owner-recognisable content.** The corpus has registry identity and broad bands, but nothing an owner would recognise as their own dog's habits. That content does not exist yet.

## What this commit adds (additions only)

- **`us_lookup_additions.csv`:** 31 normalised queries mapped to existing canonical ids, in the same columns as the lookup table plus a `note` column. With them applied, 38 of the 39 names match. Examples: boxer, chihuahua, pomeranian, doberman, cane corso, havanese, gsp, malinois, brittany, vizsla, collie, blue heeler.
- **`us_display_names.csv`:** 25 rows giving a plain US name for every common breed whose registry name isn't English, e.g. `deutscher-sch-ferhund` is German Shepherd and `chihuahue-o` is Chihuahua.

## Decisions still open

- **Pit Bull** (also pitbull, pittie) is not a registry breed. Options:
  - map it to American Staffordshire Terrier
  - map it to Staffordshire Bull Terrier
  - add a US-only "Pit Bull type" entity
  
  It's among the most common US shelter dogs, so the answer matters. Krish decides.
- **Wrong bands.** Until they are fixed, don't show size, exercise or the secondary type to buyers, and don't let them drive Biscuit's questions. The primary `dog_type` archetype is safe only as a light tone signal.
- **Habit content.** Owner-recognisable habits per breed are not written yet. Write them only after Krish has agreed the personalisation idea (see `docs/COWORK-HANDOVER-2026-10-15.md`), so the content serves that idea.

## How to apply

Load `us_lookup_additions.csv` as an overlay checked before `breed_lookup_normalized.csv`, or append its rows (drop the `note` column). Use `us_display_names.csv` for every name a buyer can see. Keep the readme's rule: unknown names fall back to the family or mixed-breed row, never to a near spelling.
