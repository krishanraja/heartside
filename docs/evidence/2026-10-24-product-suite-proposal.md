# Heartside product suite: launch, test and defer

October 24, 2026. Recommendation for Krish, not an approved catalog change. USD throughout. No products, prices, fulfillment routes or live artwork were changed for this research.

## 1. The decision

Build three core gift families: **portrait, personality blanket and mug**. Sell the portrait in both unframed and framed formats. Qualify a replacement ornament as a seasonal addition. Develop one pillow as a limited test. Keep the compact book on the 2027 development list and defer the calendar and broad novelty range.

This changes the original range. It adds an accessible wall-art format, reopens pricing, changes the ornament supplier decision and gives the pillow a fair test. It does not imply that the original four products were agreed after the research. The October 23 report remains historical evidence; the later approved city artwork and word-free personality blanket supersede its older creative suggestions.

“Launch candidate” means selected for completion and acceptance, not currently ready to sell. The core is four physical formats in three families. The ornament is a fifth format only if its gates pass. The pillow is a separate limited test, not an extra hero product.

This supports the North Star by giving people recognizable, gift-worthy objects with enough contribution to serve customers and fund growth. The main trade-off is price: high margins and today's low prices are not simultaneously established. The proposed higher prices need purchase evidence.

## 4. Margins and the price decision

The table models one item per order with free US shipping. Base costs are October 24 connected catalog values. Shipping is the official public US starting/category rate: mug $6.69, sherpa $13.99, frame $10.89, unframed $4.99, filled pillow $10.89, ornament $5.89. The 12 x 18 unframed $4.99 category was also read in the Codex browser with USD selected. Final destination quotes remain required, especially for the larger textiles and non-contiguous US addresses. Do not convert the site's default GBP table or confuse single-sided ornament shipping with this two-sided SKU.

Modeled contribution subtracts product, shipping, 2.9% + $0.30 payment processing, $0.40 Teeinblue per item, $0.40 holiday surcharge, $0.50 AI allowance, $2 QC labor allowance and 3% of retail as a remake/refund reserve. Formula: **0.941 × retail − base − shipping − $3.60**. Payment processing is an assumption, Teeinblue fee comes from the October 23 dashboard observation, and AI/labor/reserve are planning allowances. The observed three-request AI sample is too small to set a reliable per-order cost. This excludes acquisition, subscriptions, overhead and any unrecovered tax. It is not net profit or actual realized margin.

Red oak's connected frame base is $32.77, $1.73 below black/white. Keep a common retail price for a simpler choice. The blanket's $99 scenario barely clears a proposed 40% contribution floor, so it needs a $109 sensitivity test or a lower delivered cost if actual QC/retries exceed the allowance. These prices are proposed economics, not demonstrated willingness to pay. West & Willow's $40 mug makes $39 plausible, but does not validate it for Heartside.

At $19, the current ornament leaves only $5.38 after base and shipping, before other costs. For an incremental add-on that reuses artwork, assume no new AI or QC, no additional fixed payment charge and no additional Printful holiday charge: $19 − $13.62 − $0.40 − 5.9% × $19 = **$3.86**, or 20.3%. A second supplier order or extra work can reduce that further. The discount is not a high-margin offer.

For a $29 replacement ornament, a 40% contribution target permits **at most $12.09 in combined base and shipping** under the same conservative overhead model. Printify's advertised starting combination is $9.69, but it is not an exact quote for our configuration. Keep the $29 seasonal candidate conditional on both its quote and sample. Do not buy a subscription to make an unproven margin look better.

For a $79 hardcover book, allowing $3 AI, $5 QC, $0.40 personalization and $0.30 payment fixed cost plus the same percentage assumptions permits about **$34.04 delivered supplier cost** at 40% contribution. For a $59 softcover, the ceiling is **$23.22**. These are maximum acceptable quotes, not supplier prices. The calendar's advertised minimum base/shipping sum is $20.98; even before charging extra for thirteen artworks, $29 leaves only about $2.71 under the simple single-image model. That is a poor launch priority.

Forty percent is my proposed pre-acquisition contribution floor, not a previously approved business target. Recalculate with actual fees, retry rates, review time, taxes and customer acquisition costs before claiming high margins. See the accompanying CSV for reproducible scenarios.

## 5. One creative system across the selected range

The user's shared style and sentimental-to-hilarious control applies to every selected product. The six style candidates are Cinematic, Painterly, Vintage screenprint, Comic book, Cut-paper collage and Fine ink drawing. None is automatically production approved because a concept board looks good.

Use one dog photo and the facts already collected by Buddy. Carry the same identity, selected style and tone between products without asking again. City is optional and collected once. Default to a recommended style; expose the alternatives as a small visual choice. Tone has three reliable stops: Sentimental, Warm with a wink and Hilarious. Commit a selection before regeneration instead of generating on every slider movement.

Style changes rendering. Tone changes the story, expression, props and copy within the owner's facts. It must never change coat markings, ear shape, muzzle, age or dog identity. Hilarious does not mean generic sunglasses or inventing a habit. If the dog steals socks, that can become a playful visual; if no such fact exists, do not add it.

| Format | Adaptation of the shared system |
|---|---|
| Portrait, framed/unframed | Scene artwork, optional city, restrained handwritten note and signature/paw. Same artwork across frame choice. |
| Blanket | Word-free all-over faces/poses and personality motifs, soft or irregular edges. Tone is expressed visually; do not force text back onto it. |
| Mug | Readable dog identity, one coherent scene or pattern that works around the curve, short dog-voice note. Avoid shrinking an entire poster onto a mug. |
| Ornament | Large recognizable head/scene on the front; brief readable note on a separately printable reverse. Match the buyer's style and tone without copying every detail. |
| Pillow test | Dog and personality motifs composed for a square, with seam-safe areas. Do not assume a successful blanket crop is a successful pillow. |

Proposed minimum qualification: six varied dog photos across all six styles, including dark coats, light coats, distinctive markings, long hair, short muzzles and mixed breeds. Each style must preserve the individual dog's identity at all three tone stops before it appears on the storefront. Inspect the native generated source and final file at physical print size, then the actual cart/order handoff. Each style also needs a layout check on every offered format. This is a screening set, not statistical proof of universal accuracy.

If identity fails, offer the source photo treatment or a controlled retry; never silently ship a substitute dog. Preserve the last valid result through style/tone changes and reject stale image/context combinations. The existing source-resolution and private-product connection questions remain Monday support dependencies. No new paid image generation was performed for this report.

## 6. Cheap tests, acceptance and the next queue

Proposed tests are decision rules, not observed results. Small tests supply direction and failure discovery, not a market-wide conclusion.

| Uncertainty | Smallest useful test | Decision rule |
|---|---|---|
| Actual-dog likeness and tone | Existing consented/test photos in the six-dog set; compare source and output, then have owners identify their dog and the represented habit | Any identity substitution blocks that result. Repeated failure in a style blocks that style until corrected. |
| Unframed adds buyers or merely replaces framed sales | One portrait page with clearly priced framed/unframed choices; same acquisition source and artwork | Judge total contribution per qualified visitor and completed paid orders, not the unframed selection rate alone. |
| Higher prices reduce demand too much | After artwork acceptance, compare price arms with consistent traffic and presentation; cap a directional first read at 100 qualified sessions per arm or 14 days | Report the actual order counts and uncertainty. Too few purchases is inconclusive. Do not run seven simultaneous tests with scarce traffic. |
| Pillow is delightful or unwanted decor | Show finished concept and real $59 price to ten relevant owners/recipients, record intended room/use and objections; then at most ten paid pilot orders after sample acceptance | Stated liking is screening only. Graduation needs real purchases, acceptable physical quality and recipient use feedback. |
| New ornament is better | Exact US quote and print-area mapping first; compare legibility/packaging physically in the final sample batch | Misses quality, US delivery or delivered cost ceiling: leave out of this launch. Do not revert automatically to the old $19 add-on. |
| Book earns further work | Private short story/page prototype, explicit proposed price, feedback from ten relevant buyers | Advance only if the actual-dog story is valued and the selected supplier quote fits. No book development that delays core products. |
