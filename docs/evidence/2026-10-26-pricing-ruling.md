## Final owner ruling

Krish explicitly said: "I am happy to take 30% net margin at its lowest if we think WTP vs net margin will yield the highest total profits" and then directed that the main Heartside chat receive the handoff, "making clear that 30% net is the floor and the aim is maximum total profit, with the provisional products flagged."

Both user messages were independently read from the pricing research conversation. This is the later policy instruction, replacing the mandatory October 24 minimum of50% and any interim 20% downside allowance. Earlier reasoning and calculations remain historical evidence. The objective is maximum total net operating profit subject to a 30% net operating margin floor, not maximum price, margin percentage or conversion alone.

Trade-off: lower percentage margin may yield greater total profit through additional retained demand. Counterpoint: incremental acquisition, support, generation and capacity costs can consume the apparent gain. Actual net results are unverified. Do not treat unknown costs as zero or supplier-only margin as net. Per-offer and whole-business checks are a conservative implementation guardrail, not an owner-specified accounting-allocation method.

This is a venture-local implementation record, not verified canonical Decision Ledger persistence. No external ledger write or durable-memory update is claimed. Review the policy's application after each bounded price test, the first 20 fulfilled orders, monthly, or a material cost change; if conservative or realized economics fall below 30%, review the affected offer before further promotion while honoring paid orders. Changing the owner's floor remains his decision.
