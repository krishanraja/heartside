Build pack for Claude Code and for Krish. Written 4 October 2026. This replaces the ornament-led plan in `docs/COWORK-PROMPT.md` and the quiet-editorial art direction in `docs/BRIEF.md` section 7. ## 1. Why this exists (read before touching anything)

Krish looked at the first redesign and said it had no personality, no urgency, no emotion and no "take my money" feeling. He was right. Research on 4 October showed why. The pet-gift category is a wall of identical ads: "watercolor portrait of your pet on a mug / doormat / blanket", from Macorner, Callie, Joymemento, Pawsionate and hundreds more. A photo of your dog on a thing is a commodity. Etsy sells photo pet ornaments for $6–8.

What sells in this category is the dog turned into something with a point of view. Crown & Paw made $10M in year one with pets as royalty. Pupsentials made $5M in two years with embroidered pet sweatshirts. The strongest long-running ad in the Meta Ad Library was a story ad that opens on how many photos of the dog the owner has, a specific, funny truth.

Nobody owns the dog's side of the relationship. Heartside's positioning was already "gifts for people whose dog is their person". v2 makes the dog the author of every gift. The dog runs your household like a deadpan middle manager, and every product is something it issued to you.

**The feeling we want, in order:** a laugh out loud in the first two seconds, then "that is exactly my dog", then the tender turn at the end ("You also came back. Every single time. I noticed."), then the urge to send it to someone. Hilarity is the delivery system and tenderness is the payload. Never let the joke become mean about the human or the dog.

**Brand sign-off:** "Your dog has notes." **Tagline, unchanged:** "Keep them close." It appears once, at the end, where the tender turn lands.

## 2. The comedy bible (for site copy, ads, emails, packaging)

**The world.** Heartside Household Inc. The dog is Head of Household. The human is "My Person", an employee under annual review. There is HR paperwork (Form HR-26), incident reports, a dress code, surveillance, a benefits package, holiday office closure notices and memos from management.

**The dog's voice.** Flat, corporate and completely sincere. Short sentences. The dog never knows it is funny. It uses office language for dog things ("Strong numbers.", "We have discussed this.", "Case closed.", "Under investigation."). It is petty about small betrayals (the vacuum, the mailman, leaving the house, baths) and quietly devoted underneath.

**Joke patterns that work.**
- Corporate frame applied to a dog crime: "The sock. I would do it again. Case closed."
- Precise, absurd numbers the owner supplies: "Opened the treat cupboard 1,412 times."
- The flat one-word escalation: "Allegedly." / "Security." / "Who is Brenda."
- The long silence before a small complaint: "The vacuum. I'm not going to say anything. ... I'm very disappointed."

**Never.** Never "it's not X, it's Y", "Not X. Y.", or staccato false binaries. No emoji in headlines, no exclamation marks in body copy, no "must-have", "elevate", "unleash", "game-changer". No jokes about the dog dying, getting old, being ill, or being abandoned. No mocking body types, breeds or the customer. No fake countdown timers.

## 5. Fulfilment (as close to hands-off as possible, with one human quality gate)

**Stack:** Shopify, the Printful app (free) and Teeinblue (personalization, $49/month plus per-item). Both installs need Krish's yes.

**Why Teeinblue:** it is the only piece that does all four hard things for photo products. It removes the background and cuts out the dog (needed for the pillow, socks, sweatshirt and bandana). It shows a live preview on the product page. It renders print-ready files from a template. It sends the order to Printful automatically. Building that ourselves would take longer than the 16 days to launch. Customily ($49/month) is the fallback with the same integrations.

**Flow per order.**
1. The customer personalises on the product page: the dog's name, their name, the treat-cupboard number, picks from the joke options (the same chips as the homepage), and uploads a photo or chooses "send it later".
2. Teeinblue generates the print file and creates the Printful order **on hold**.
3. **Quality gate:** Krish (or a Cowork session) opens the Printful dashboard once a day, checks each design (crop, cutout, spelling, photo resolution) and confirms it. This is the "a human checks every order" promise on the site. It must stay true.
4. Printful prints, ships and syncs tracking back to Shopify. The customer gets Shopify's shipping email, rewritten in the dog's voice.
5. **Video add-on:** a daily Cowork session pulls that day's video orders, generates each 15-second clip with Higgsfield from the customer's photo and review text, and emails it from the store. Promise "within 48 hours". Cap it at 40 videos a day by setting inventory, because that is the real limit of the process.

**Homepage generator → product page.** The homepage review builder is our own theme section (no app). Its "Approve [Dog]'s review" button opens the product page with the answers in URL parameters. Claude Code must check whether Teeinblue can prefill its fields from URL parameters or a JS API. If it can't, the product page repeats the choices in Teeinblue's own customizer, styled to match. Do not ship a flow where the customer has to type everything twice without telling Krish.

**Quality before launch.** See section 8, item 2. If Krish orders the two samples, check the pillow cutout edges and the sweatshirt face alignment, and photograph both for the site. Every other product image comes from Printful's mockup generator, using the real design. Do not use mockups that look better than the real thing.

**Christmas deadlines.** Printful's verified 2026 cutoff for US-made products on standard shipping is 11 December. Publish one day earlier per product to cover the daily review step:

| Product | Publish as last order date | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Poster, framed, socks, ornament, pillow | 10 December | Printful US cutoff 11 Dec verified; confirm per product in the Printful dashboard |
| Uniform sweatshirt and bandana | [DATE] | Printful's all-over-print facility and its cutoff must be confirmed before publishing |
| Video | 23 December | Our own process |

Nothing goes on the site until each date is confirmed in the Printful dashboard.

## 6. The ads (AI-made with Higgsfield; scripts are on the canvas)

**The recurring character:** Biscuit, a miniature dachshund, Head of Household. Generate an **original** character in Higgsfield as a character sheet, not from the stock hero photo, whose licence is still unconfirmed. Keep him identical across every ad. Reading glasses for HR scenes, a tiny black turtleneck for the heist. Give him one voice: a flat, sincere, middle-aged American office manager. Create it once with Higgsfield's voice tool and pass it as the audio reference on every clip, so he always sounds the same.

**Production recipe.**
- **Model:** Seedance 2.5, mode `omni_reference`, 9:16. Image references: the Biscuit character sheet and the product shot.
- **Audio:** `generate_audio` on, with the dialogue line written into the prompt.
- **Iterate cheaply, then finalise:** use `draft: true` (480p) until the take is funny, then finalise the chosen draft at 1080p.
- **Shots:** one shot per line, 3–6 seconds each. Assemble with Higgsfield's video-montage workflow.
- **Captions:** burn them in, because most people watch on mute.
- **End card on every ad:** the product, then "Your dog has notes. heartside.io".
- **Hooks:** make three versions of each ad's first 2 seconds.

**The five ads** (full frame-by-frame on the canvas):
1. **The Review (20s):** Biscuit runs Sarah's annual review across a mahogany desk. "You left the house 41 times." "For work." "Allegedly." Sells the poster and framed poster.
2. **The Body Double (15s):** a heist. Biscuit plants his pillow double next to Sarah on the sofa and goes for the Christmas ham. "You're so calm tonight." Sells the pillow.
3. **Mandatory Company Uniform (20s):** the living-room holiday party. Everyone is in Biscuit sweatshirts. Uncle Dave walks in wearing a plain sweater. "Security." Two bulldogs with earpieces walk him out. Sells the set.
4. **Surveillance Socks (12s):** Sarah kicks her shoes off at the office. Cut to Biscuit at a wall of CCTV monitors. "Who is Brenda." Sells socks.
5. **Your dog reads your review (15s):** a UGC-style phone video. A woman holds her phone next to her confused real dog while the dog's photo reads her review aloud. "You went to Cabo without me. One star." Sells the video, and with it the poster.

**The tender cut, for retargeting only:** the "word from management" memo read over quiet footage, ending "You also came back. Every single time. I noticed. Keep me close."

**Distribution.** Post organically first: TikTok, Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts, two posts a day from 13 October, as the Heartside account and as Biscuit's own account. After 72 hours, put the $100–150 behind only the two clips with the highest share and save rates, using TikTok Spark Ads and Meta boosts. Pin a comment on every post asking "Who would your dog fire?" or "Who is your dog's manager?". Replies with dog photos are free reach.

**Disclosure:** state that the ads are AI-generated where TikTok and Meta require it. Use their AI-content labels.

## 10. Decisions log (made in this pack, with the reason)

- **Hero product: the Annual Review poster.** Highest margin (54%), fastest to make, the most shareable output, and the funniest thing to personalise.
- **Printful as the single maker.** One shipment and one set of cutoffs. US production for nearly everything.
- **30% off retired for the new range; the free video is the launch offer.** It protects margin and drives the share loop.
- **Original AI Biscuit in ads, never the stock dachshund photo.** The photo's licence is unconfirmed, and one character lets the brand compound.
- **Organic first, paid only behind proven clips.** The ad budget is $100–150, and break-even per purchase is $21–60.
