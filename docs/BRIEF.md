## 1. The north star

### The feeling we are building

A person is on the sofa on a Sunday evening. Their small dog has climbed onto their chest, turned around twice, and gone to sleep with its chin on the collarbone. The person has stopped scrolling their phone. They can feel a heartbeat that is not their own, quick and light, about twice the speed of theirs. They are not thinking about anything. It is the best minute of their week.

Heartside is the brand for that minute.

When someone lands on the site, watches an ad, or opens a parcel, they should feel the small ache of how much they love their dog, and then the warmth of being understood. The emotion is tenderness with a little bit of time pressure folded in: small dogs live short lives compared with ours, everyone who loves one knows it, and nobody says it. We never say it either. We make the days feel held. We do not use grief, loss or fear to sell anything.

### The promise

**Keep them close.**

The brand line already on the site is "The sling that keeps your dog close". The product line becomes the brand line. Heartside is about proximity: the dog on your heart side, the walk where you scoop them up when their legs are tired, the name on the tag that comes home with them, the gift that says "I know who your person is."

### The name

Heartside is the side of you where the heart is, and the side your dog chooses. Est. 2022 sits under the logo; keep it.

### What the brand believes

- A small dog is a whole person to the human who loves it.
- Carrying your dog is love, not convenience.
- The right gift for a dog parent is one that is about the dog and shows you noticed.
- Premium shows up as attention to detail and warmth.

### What we refuse to be

- A pet-store catalogue of cheap, cluttered, shouting products.
- A fear brand ("don't let your dog get hurt"). No safety claims, no health claims, no anxiety claims.
- A fake-urgency brand. The 30% offer ends on 1 November. That is real, so the countdown is real. Nothing else is invented.
- A brand with invented reviews, invented customers or invented numbers. Ever.

### Voice

Quiet, specific, warm, slightly wry. Short sentences carry the feeling; one longer sentence is allowed per paragraph. Speaks to one person ("you", "your dog"), uses the dog's name whenever the customer has given it. Details beat adjectives: "a chin on the collarbone" beats "super cosy".

Never write these patterns anywhere in copy, ads or emails: "It's not X, it's Y", "Not X. Y.", and staccato false binaries such as "Two options. One choice." Say the plain declarative thing. Also avoid "elevate", "game-changer", "unleash", "must-have", exclamation marks in body copy, and emoji in headlines.

## 2. Why the plan changed

The first plan was a single product (a neoprene dog sling) priced high-ish, with a small range around it. We tested that against reality on 4 October 2026 and it did not hold.

Evidence gathered:

- **Amazon prices.** Searching "dog sling carrier small dog" on Amazon shows hundreds of results. The best seller (Tomkas, over 32,000 reviews, 2,000+ bought in the last month) costs about £10. Most slings cost £7 to £38 with fast delivery. The only items above £40 are rare.
- **Our cost.** The sling we source costs $34.45 plus $6.00 shipping, so about $40.45 landed. At a $99.99 list price with 30% off ($69.99), the margin is about $27 before ads. At a price that matches mainstream expectations it is under $14.
- **Meta Ad Library (US, active ads, "dog sling carrier").** About 24 results. Three comparable advertisers: Hunters Doggy Shop (running several creatives since late August and mid-September, offering 50% off), Pawpubby (running since 26 August), and Callie Gifts (many creatives since 23 September, selling a personalised sling as a birthday and gift item). The rest are rear-support slings for senior dogs (heavy spenders, heavy health claims, excluded by our rules) and baby carriers.
- **Stock.** Only the Charcoal variant of the sling has stock (54 units). Pink and Beige are at 0. The photos on the site lead with a cream sling the customer cannot buy.
- **Dropshipping reality.** The suppliers are marketplace sellers with 1 to 14 day shipping. Amazon is faster and cheaper on the commodity. A $100 to $150 ad test buys a handful of orders, so it can tell us whether the page and creative work. It cannot prove demand.

### What follows

A commodity cannot beat Amazon on price. The one place a small brand escapes price comparison is gifting and personalisation, where the buyer is buying a gesture and the dog's name is on it. Callie Gifts' personalised sling ads are the only competitor signal pointing that way.

So the new position, **recommended and agreed in principle with Krish**:

> Heartside makes gifts for people whose small dog is their person.

The hero shifts from the sling alone to a gift story in which personalisation does the work the sling cannot: the **Heartside Tag** (a tag with the dog's name) is the easy first gift, the **sling** is the premium centrepiece, and **bundles** are where margin lives.

Krish's own instinct, "bougie expensive gifts for dogs at Christmas", is the direction. The caveats that came with it: Christmas shipping cutoffs, Q4 ad costs, and the fact that premium needs to look and feel premium. This brief answers each.

## 3. Customer and moments

### Primary customer

The dog parent, 28 to 55, who treats a small or senior dog as family. They talk to the dog. They own a dog-shaped something in every room. They buy gifts for other dog people at Christmas and for themselves in between.

### Secondary customer

The gift giver: a partner, parent, sibling or friend who needs a present for someone whose dog is their world. They are anxious about getting it wrong and short on time. They need a clear "this is the gift" answer, a gift message, and an honest "order by" date.

### Moments to sell into (each is an ad angle and a page section)

1. **Sunday on the chest.** The dog asleep on you. (Hero emotion; the brand line.)
2. **The last quarter mile.** The walk where a small dog slows down and you scoop them up, and the walk carries on. (Sling.)
3. **From the dog.** A Christmas gift that arrives "from" the dog, with their name on it. (Tag, gift sets.)
4. **Gotcha day.** The anniversary of the day a rescue dog came home. (Tag, gift sets.)
5. **The first night.** A new puppy's first week. (Tag, sling.)
6. **The dog person who has everything.** The friend you buy for every year. (Gift sets with a printed letter.)

Do not build anything around memorial or loss. It is tempting emotional territory and it is the wrong thing to sell into.

**Price floor rule:** never sell any item or bundle for less than landed cost + 3% + $0.30 + $8 contribution, with or without the 30% discount. If a price fails that rule, raise the list price. Do not remove a product from the discount quietly.

- The homepage banner currently says "50% off until Nov 1". That is wrong. Change it to **30%**.
- After 1 November, decide whether to keep a smaller seasonal offer. Do not let the strike-through prices rely on a permanent "sale".

### The three things that make it feel premium without inventory

1. **The dog's name.** Collected in the cart ("What's their name?"), printed or engraved on the tag where the supplier allows it, and used in the confirmation email and the gift card.
2. **A letter.** Every Gift Set and Carry Set includes a downloadable and printable "letter to your dog's person" or a gift card the buyer can forward by email or print. This costs nothing to ship and is the part people remember. Write four variants (see section 9).
3. **Presentation.** A calm, warm, editorial site; considered confirmation and shipping emails; the same watercolour heart on everything. Physical packaging is outside our control with dropshipping, so the digital layer has to carry the premium feel. Do not promise gift wrapping, tissue paper or a branded box unless a supplier genuinely offers it.

## 5. Christmas timing

Everything here must be verified with the suppliers before it is printed on the site.

- Launch target: **20 October 2026**.
- The 30% offer ends **1 November**.
- Black Friday weekend is **27 to 30 November**; ad costs spike from then.
- Typical US carrier cutoffs for Christmas delivery fall in the **third week of December**, but suppliers with 1 to 3 day shipping plus processing time need earlier dates. Personalised items need extra production days.
- **Recommended on-site wording:** "Order by 10 December for Christmas delivery" for 1-3 day items, and a clearly earlier date for any personalised item once the supplier confirms production time. Do not publish a date the supplier has not confirmed.
- Put the cutoff on the product page, in the cart, in the checkout notice and in the emails. A gift buyer needs it more than a discount.

## 6. The economics, stated plainly

- **Budget:** $100 to $150 for the paid test. Krish treats the whole thing as a side-income test and has a cap of about six hours of build time.
- **Break-even cost per sale** for the bundles above is roughly $10 (Tag), $14 (Sling), $17 (Carry Set) and $21 (Gift Set). A typical paid-social cost per purchase in Q4 is likely to be higher than that on a cold audience. That is why the plan is **organic-first**.
- **What the test can tell us:** whether the page converts (add-to-cart rate, checkout starts, purchase rate), which hook works, and the cost of a click. It cannot prove long-term demand with this budget.
- **Organic first, paid second.** Post the video ads as ordinary content on TikTok and Instagram Reels for roughly two weeks before spending money. Spend only behind the one or two videos that earn saves, shares and comments. Krish is strong at content and AI production; this is where he has the advantage.
- **Suggested decision rules (recommended, adjustable):**
  - Spend $15 to $20 a day on the best organic video for five to seven days.
  - If there are no add-to-carts after about 300 landing-page sessions, fix the page or the hook before spending more.
  - If a bundle sells at a cost per sale below its margin, add budget in steps of 20%.
  - If nothing converts by 1 December, stop. Treat the result as information, not failure.
- **Stock cap.** Do not run paid traffic beyond what supplier stock supports. The sling has 54 units. Confirm restock terms with the supplier before scaling.

## 7. Visual direction

### The one-line art direction

**A warm, quiet, editorial pet brand shot at golden hour: soft light, close crops, real tenderness, and one watercolour heart as the only decoration.**

Think of a hand-written note tucked into a good magazine. The site should feel like that: quiet, personal and unhurried, a long way from a pet-shop aisle or a loud template store.

### Colour

Sampled from the supplied logo and heart:

| Token | Value | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `--ink` | `#121010` | Wordmark, headlines, primary button text on blush |
| `--blush` | `#F0C0C0` | Soft backgrounds, hover fills, card tints |
| `--rose` | `#E49CB4` | Accents, links on light, focus rings, the heart |
| `--peach` | `#F0B4A8` | Secondary accent, gradients with rose |
| `--cream` | `#FBF6F1` | Page background |
| `--chestnut` | `#8A4A2B` | Warm text accent, drawn from the dachshund photo |
| `--paper` | `#FFFFFF` | Cards |

Rules:
- Background is cream, not pure white and not grey.
- The primary button is **ink** with cream text, with a blush hover state. The current bright orange hero button (`#FF7A00`) clashes with the palette and must go.
- Use rose and peach as light washes and accents, never as large saturated blocks.
- Check text contrast: ink on cream passes easily; rose text on cream does not, so use rose for graphics and large type only.

### Typography

The logo is a high-contrast display serif with long, curling ascenders (the "h" and "d"). Echo it, do not copy it.

- **Headlines:** a refined high-contrast serif. Recommended: Cormorant Garamond (light or regular, large sizes) or Fraunces (soft, optical size set high). Sentence case. Generous size and line height. One italic word allowed per headline for emphasis.
- **Body and UI:** a quiet humanist sans. Recommended: DM Sans or Instrument Sans, 16 to 18 px body, 1.6 line height.
- The wordmark stays as the supplied image. Never retype it in a font.

### Imagery

Principles:
- **Eye level with the dog.** Faces close to the lens. The supplied dachshund photo (chin on the floor, looking up) is the reference for emotional intensity.
- **Golden-hour or window light.** Warm, directional, with natural shadow. No flat studio lighting or white cut-outs on the homepage.
- **The human is a body, not a model.** Hands, a collarbone, a shoulder strap, a coat sleeve. Faces of owners are fine but never the focus.
- **Texture and grain.** A light film grain and soft colour grade toward warm cream and rose.
- **Real scale.** Small dogs look small. Show the dog's weight in the arms.
- **Product shots** sit in a lived-in scene, not a void.

Avoid: stock-photo smiles at the camera, bright primary colours, dogs in costumes, harsh flash, and anything that looks like a supermarket pet aisle.

### The watercolour heart

The supplied heart is the signature. Use it:
- Behind or beside the wordmark (as in the logo).
- As the favicon and the loading animation (a slow pulse).
- As the divider between sections, small and quiet.
- As the "sold out" and "thank you" mark.
Do not use it as a repeating pattern, a drop shadow or a background texture at full opacity. One heart at a time.

### Motion and sound

- **Heartbeat pace.** Animations ease slowly. The pulse of the heart on the site and in video runs at about 100 to 120 beats per minute (a small dog's resting heart rate, roughly double a human's). Never faster than that, and never frantic.
- **Page motion:** fade and gentle rise on scroll, 400 to 600 ms, no bouncing, no parallax gimmicks.
- **Sound design for video:** a soft, low heartbeat under the opening seconds, room tone, fabric sounds, paws on wood, and one piece of sparse piano or acoustic guitar. No stock "upbeat ukulele".

### Layout

- Lots of white space. Content width about 1,100 px; text width about 640 px.
- Large rounded corners (16 to 24 px) on cards and images.
- Full-bleed photography for the hero and one mid-page "feeling" section.
- Mobile first. Most traffic will come from TikTok and Instagram on a phone.

### Reference points (for mood, not to copy)

Editorial lifestyle brands with a warm, tactile feel: think the calm pacing of high-end bedding, candle or stationery brands, and the photographic warmth of premium pet and baby brands. Krish should add 3 to 5 specific references here when he has them.

## 10. Guardrails

These are firm. If an instruction elsewhere conflicts with a guardrail, the guardrail wins and the conflict gets reported to Krish.

1. **No fake reviews, testimonials, customer counts or "as seen in" claims.** FTC rules apply to the US market, and these sink small stores. Leave review sections empty until real customers fill them.
2. **No safety, health or anxiety claims.** That covers "safety strap", "keeps your dog safe", "reduces anxiety", "calming", "supports joints" and anything similar. Describe what the product is and does mechanically.
3. **Accurate product claims.** Materials, sizes, weight limits and washing instructions must match what the supplier states. The sling is neoprene; the supplier listing says "organic neoprene", which cannot be verified, so do not repeat it. The listing says "for dogs and cats up to 15 lbs"; we say dogs.
4. **Honest scarcity and time.** The 1 November countdown is real. Stock counts shown must be real. No resetting timers.
5. **AI-generated video must not misrepresent the product.** Order sample units of every product before filming or generating. Show the real sling, the real tag, the real bottle. Follow Meta and TikTok rules on labelling AI-generated or AI-altered content, and add a label where the platform requires one.
6. **Persona boundary.** Krish's personal email address must not appear on the site, in policies, in ads, in emails or in this repo. Use a Heartside address on customer-facing material (see section 11), or `hello@krishraja.com` only if no Heartside address exists. `krishraja.com` is his site.
7. **No clothing, no electrical items, no food or supplements, no bulky items, no memorial-themed selling.** These were excluded earlier for returns, certification, regulatory or shipping reasons. The Floral Dog Jacket, the Puff Parka and the Golden Spike Collar are in the store today and should be archived.
8. **Do not change store-level settings without Krish.** That includes currency, payments, markets, tax and domains.
9. **Do not publish or launch.** Claude Code prepares everything. Krish presses go.
