# Heartside: Video Ad Brief

For Claude Code, Higgsfield and Krish. Read `BRIEF.md` first, especially section 1 (the feeling), section 7 (visual direction) and section 10 (guardrails). This document turns them into videos.

---

## 1. What the videos must do

Make a dog parent feel the small ache of how much they love their dog, then show them a way to act on it in the next ten seconds: a tag with their dog's name, a sling for the tired-legs walk, or a gift for another dog person.

The feeling comes first. The product arrives late and quietly. If a viewer could skip the product and still feel something, the video is working.

### The viewer, in one sentence

A woman in her thirties or forties on the sofa at 9 pm, thumbing through her phone with her small dog on her lap.

### The one thing they should do

Tap through to a gift page, with 30% off and free US shipping until 1 November, and add their dog's name.

---

## 2. Production approach

Krish produces these with AI tools. That is a strength here, but it comes with discipline.

### Order the products first

Order one unit each of the sling (Charcoal), the tag and the water bottle. Film or photograph the real products and use those references for every generation. **An AI video must not show the product doing anything it doesn't do or looking like something it isn't.** This is both an advertising-policy rule and an FTC rule, and it is the quickest route to a banned ad account.

### Tools

- **Higgsfield** for generating shots and for image-to-video. Use the real product photos as reference images.
- **A real dog if possible.** If Krish or a friend has a small dog, shoot 30 minutes of real footage: chin on collarbone, paws, the doorway, the walk. Real footage is the strongest asset and makes the AI shots look better by contrast. If not, build a consistent AI dog (see 3).
- **Captions** burned in on every video. Most viewers watch with sound off for the first seconds.

### Disclosure

If a video contains realistic synthetic people or dogs, apply the AI-content label each platform requires (Meta's "AI info" and TikTok's AI-generated content label). Do it every time. A generated video that implies real customers, real testimonials or real results is not allowed (see BRIEF section 10).

### Specs

- 9:16, 1080 x 1920. Safe zone: keep captions and the product away from the top 250 px and bottom 400 px (platform UI).
- Primary cut: 15 seconds. Cutdowns: 6 seconds (hook plus CTA) and 30 seconds (full story) for each concept.
- Three hook variants per concept: change only the first 2 seconds.
- Audio: low heartbeat, room tone, fabric, paws, one sparse piano or acoustic guitar track. Royalty-free or generated; log the licence.
- File naming: `hs_[concept]_[hook]_[length]_v[version].mp4`, for example `hs_chest_h2_15s_v1.mp4`.

## 3. The hero dog (character bible)

Consistent visuals matter more than variety. Pick one dog and keep it for all content.

- **Recommended:** a small smooth-haired dachshund with a chestnut coat and a tan face, to match the supplied brand photograph (`assets/hero-dachshund.png`): amber eyes, a slightly worried brow, big soft ears.
- **Name:** use a placeholder name on screen ("Biscuit") until Krish picks one. The name matters because the tag concept is built around it.
- **Size:** under 15 lbs. Show weight and scale honestly: a small dog in two arms, not a toy.
- **Prompt anchor** (use the same text in every generation, then add the scene): "small chestnut-and-tan smooth-haired dachshund, amber eyes, soft long ears, warm natural light, shallow depth of field, cinematic, 35mm film grain, photoreal".
- Keep the owner mostly out of frame (hands, collarbone, shoulder, a cream knit jumper). When a face is needed, keep it soft and partial.

## 4. The concepts

Each concept lists the hook, the beats, the on-screen text, the sound and the call to action. Times are for the 15-second cut.

### Concept A: "Sunday on the chest" (hero film, the brand in 15 seconds)

**Why:** this is the north-star feeling and the existing homepage line.

| Time | Picture | Sound | On-screen text |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-2 | Extreme close-up: a dog's chin resting on a collarbone, a chest rising slowly | Low heartbeat, then a very soft inhale | *(none)* |
| 2-6 | Slow pull-back: the dog asleep on a person's chest on a sofa, window light | Heartbeat continues, piano begins | "Some of the best moments of your day" |
| 6-9 | Hand gently resting on the dog's back | Fabric sound | "are the ones where your loved one is asleep on your heart." |
| 9-12 | The dog now in a charcoal sling against a person's chest, walking down a leafy street at golden hour | Piano lifts slightly | "Heartside. Keep them close." |
| 12-15 | Product + tag close-up, then the heart logo | Heartbeat fades | "Gifts for people whose dog is their person. 30% off + free US shipping until 1 Nov." |

**Hook variants (first 2 seconds):**
1. The close-up above.
2. A single line of text on black, "Your dog is asleep on you right now, isn't she?", then cut to the chin shot.
3. The heart pulsing on cream, then a cut to the dog.

### Concept B: "From the dog" (Christmas gift)

**Why:** the personalised gift angle is the commercial core. This is the video most likely to earn shares.

| Time | Picture | Sound | On-screen text |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-2 | A hand slides a small card toward the camera, reading "From Biscuit" | Soft paper sound | "A gift from the dog." |
| 2-6 | A person unwraps a small parcel at a kitchen table, Christmas lights soft in the background | Piano, gentle laugh | *(none)* |
| 6-10 | Close-up on the tag with the dog's name, held on a finger, then clipped to a collar | Click of the clasp | "Their name. Their person." |
| 10-13 | The dog nuzzling the person's hand | Warm room tone | "The gift for the dog person who has everything." |
| 13-15 | Order screen showing the name field being typed; logo | Heartbeat | "Add their name. Order by 10 Dec.*" |

*The date must be confirmed with the suppliers before this ships.

**Hook variants:** (1) the card, (2) a text hook on black: "Who do you buy for when their dog is their whole world?", (3) a hand typing the dog's name.

### Concept C: "The last quarter mile" (the sling)

**Why:** it makes the sling feel like love, not equipment, and shows the product doing exactly what it does.

| Time | Picture | Sound | On-screen text |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-3 | A small dog trotting on a lead; at the corner, his steps slow down | Paws on pavement, then slower | "Little legs. Big walks." |
| 3-6 | A hand scoops the dog up | Soft "oof" of a lift | *(none)* |
| 6-10 | The dog settles into the charcoal sling against the person's chest; the walk continues | Piano, heartbeat | "The walk doesn't have to end." |
| 10-13 | Close-ups: the pocket (a phone slides in), the strap, the dog's chin over the edge | Fabric | "Hands free. Up to 15 lbs." |
| 13-15 | Logo and offer | Heartbeat | "Heartside. 30% off until 1 Nov." |

Do not add any claim about the dog being safe, calm or protected. The pictures show the carrying; the text stays factual.

### Concept D: "Doorway" (reunion)

**Why:** universal and shareable. Low production cost.

A door opens. The dog's whole body wags. The owner kneels. A beat of stillness as the dog presses into her chest. Text: "Every day is a homecoming." The tag jingles. End on the heart, then the offer.

### Concept E: "Typing the name" (satisfying product shot)

**Why:** personalisation is the premium cue and this format performs as ASMR-style content.

Close-up of hands entering a dog's name at checkout while soft keyboard sounds play; cut to a finished tag being polished and placed into a box with a printed letter; cut to the dog. Text: "Made with their name on it." Offer card.

### Concept F: "Gotcha day" (rescue anniversary)

A short montage of a dog's first year at home, with date stamps ("Day 1", "Day 30", "Day 365") and the tag in the same place in every frame. Text: "The day they came home." End: "Mark it with their name." Only use if Krish has permission and real footage; avoid fabricating a real person's rescue story.

## 5. Test plan

### Organic first

- Post one video a day for 10 days on TikTok and Instagram Reels, rotating concepts and hooks.
- Save every post's view count, 3-second hold rate, completion rate, saves, shares and comments.
- Track which hook (first 2 seconds) earns the highest 3-second hold.
- Link in bio (and TikTok shop link if available) goes to the matching product page.

### Paid second

- Choose the one or two videos with the best saves and shares per 1,000 views.
- Start with $15 to $20 a day for five to seven days, to US audiences, broad targeting.
- Judge on add-to-carts per 100 sessions and cost per purchase against the margins in `BRIEF.md` section 4.
- Pause anything with a click-through under 0.8% after 1,000 impressions.
- Stop if nothing converts by 1 December.

### What to record

A single spreadsheet: video name, hook, platform, date, spend, impressions, 3-second hold, completion, clicks, add-to-carts, purchases, revenue. Claude Code can generate the template.

## 6. Higgsfield prompt starters

Use as starting points and adjust. Keep the hero-dog anchor from section 3 in every prompt.

- **Concept A, shot 1:** "extreme close-up of a small chestnut-and-tan smooth-haired dachshund's chin resting on a person's collarbone, a cream knit jumper, soft window light, the person's chest rising slowly, shallow depth of field, 35mm film grain, warm cream and rose colour grade, photoreal, handheld, 9:16".
- **Concept A, shot 4:** "small chestnut dachshund in a charcoal neoprene sling worn crossbody, on a leafy residential street at golden hour, the person's hand resting on the dog's back, slow walking pace, cinematic, 9:16" (use the real product photo as the reference image).
- **Concept B:** "close-up of a hand holding a small round pet ID tag engraved with the name BISCUIT, kitchen table, soft Christmas lights blurred in the background, warm light, 9:16".
- **Concept C, shot 2:** "a hand scoops up a small dachshund from a pavement, slow motion, golden hour, the dog's ears flopping, tender, 9:16".

Always review each output for: a dog with the wrong number of legs or paws, a sling worn in a way the real one is not, a tag with garbled text, and any product detail that does not match the real item. Regenerate until it does.

---

## 7. Hard rules for ad content

1. No claims about safety, health, anxiety, calming or protection.
2. No invented customers, testimonial quotes, ratings, sales numbers or "sold out" scenes.
3. Show the real product, working the way it works.
4. The offer wording matches the store exactly: 30% off, free US shipping, until 1 November.
5. Any date for Christmas delivery is confirmed with the supplier before it goes live.
6. Label AI-generated content as the platform requires.
7. No Krish personal email address, and no personal phone number, anywhere in the creative or the captions.
8. Music must be licensed for commercial use.
