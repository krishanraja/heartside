Krish's instruction (4 October 2026): pull out every stop on emotion, humour, shareability and conversion. This file turns that into the product page, the share mechanics and the videos. Read `docs/BRIEF.md` section 1 for the voice and section 10 for the guardrails. The guardrails are why this sells, so they stay (see the last section).

## The idea in one line

**Who's your person?** The front makes you go "aww" (*Biscuit is my person.*). The back makes you laugh (*Merry Christmas from the one who hogs the bed.*). Every video, product photo and caption is built on that flip.

Why it converts:

- **Owners see themselves.** Their phone is already full of photos of the dog. The ornament gives one of those photos a place on the tree, and the name makes it unmistakably theirs.
- **Givers look thoughtful.** A gift with the dog's face and name shows you noticed what matters to someone. That is what a gift buyer is paying for, and it is why they forward the product page to a sibling.
- **The humour makes it shareable.** "The one who hogs the bed" is true of most small dogs, and people tag the friend it's true of. Tags and DM sends are what the algorithms reward.
- **It's bought in multiples.** The dog's "grandma" wants one, so a second unit costs nothing to sell.

## Share mechanics that cost nothing

1. **The live preview is the ad.** Printify's personaliser shows the buyer their dog on the ornament before checkout. People screenshot that and send it to someone ("look"), so the preview has to look this good, which the heart window does.
2. **Caption CTAs ask for a tag or a send, never a like.** "Tag the person whose dog is their whole personality." "Send this to whoever calls your dog their grandchild."
3. **The flip is a second beat in every video.** Turning the ornament over gives a reveal, a laugh and a reason to rewatch. Rewatches lift completion rate.
4. **The dog's name in every customer message.** Order confirmation, shipping, review request. The brief already sets this; it matters more here because the name is printed on the product.

## Urgency, using only real deadlines

- **Until 1 November:** 30% off and free US shipping. The countdown is real, so show it everywhere: announcement bar, product page, the last frame of every video.
- **From 2 November:** the Christmas order-by date becomes the deadline, and for a gift it's the stronger one. "Order by [date] for Christmas" goes on the product page, in the cart, in the checkout notice and in captions. Use the confirmed date only.
- **The week of the order-by date:** one email and one post, "Last few days to get [Name] on the tree by Christmas." It's true, and it's the highest-intent moment of the season.

## What stays out, and why it protects sales

- **Guilt and mortality** ("they won't be here forever"), and anything about loss or memorials. It's the most tempting line for an ornament, and the brief rules it out. It would make Heartside a grief brand, and the front only works because the dog is right there on the sofa.
- **Fake scarcity** (resetting timers, "only 3 left", "selling fast"). The product is printed on demand, so any stock claim would be false. False urgency is an FTC enforcement area, and misleading claims break Meta's and TikTok's ad policies.
- **Invented reactions and reviews**, including AI-generated "customers" crying at their gift. The FTC rule on fake reviews and testimonials (in force since October 2024) carries civil penalties, and a restricted ad account sells nothing. Real reactions from real buyers, with permission, are the best content you'll get, so ask for them in the review email.
- **AI-generated people presented as real.** Label generated footage where the platform requires it (see `docs/AD-BRIEF.md` section 2).
